## 1. Introduction: the short circuit in the machine

<ZoomableImage src="/images/posts/beyond-the-chatbot/ai-wow-factor-vs-structured-context.png" alt="Illustration of the AI wow-factor breaking down into unstructured chat without context, then being filtered into structured work context" />

Put on a classic architect's glasses (durability and structural integrity) and the wow factor of Large Language Models fades once you realize that such a **digital assistant is disconnected from your actual work context**. However brilliantly these AI agents respond to our questions, they are blind to our legacy migrations, our architecture decisions, and the day-to-day decision logs that actually define our work.

Over the past while, AI mastery has stopped being about writing more poetic prompts for me. My blue DISC profile clashes with that kind of free-form prompting, and demands a **stricter approach**: one that enables AI effectiveness from its foundation up. A foundation that offers a **machine-readable base of your own work context**. If you want to promote that AI agent to a worthy colleague, you can't treat it as a chatbot — you're looking at a project to **reshape** your work context into a structured, transparent asset.

In this blog post I'll walk you through my own evolution: how my work context became usable for an AI agent, and which **safeguards** I put in place to deploy it safely.

## 2. Your habits are older than your tools

That AI effectiveness we just talked about rests on **digitizing your work context**. Your AI agent has no idea about anything you never wrote down or fed it, and hallucinates to fill the gaps.

I've been experimenting with **taking notes** since 2002. I tried thematic grouping, industrialized a [Microsoft OneNote](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/onenote/) workflow, and kept sharpening my sword until a **disciplined journaling habit** became second nature. That led me to a fixed daily rhythm: every workday gets a structured note, with fixed sections for planning and reflection. That became my second brain.

That journaling habit is today the **fuel of my AI workflow.** Alongside my own notes, I merge Copilot conversation transcripts into a "complete" daily note. I no longer need to brief my AI agent on the outcome of a design discussion: it can just read it in my journal. That structural discipline is an absolute precondition for the AI support I enjoy today.

<ZoomableImage src="/images/posts/beyond-the-chatbot/onenote-vs-markdown-tree-structuur.png" alt="Comparison of a monolithic OneNote journal versus a distributed Markdown knowledge base as a folder tree" />

That habit of faithful record-keeping, years older than my AI use, is what enables my current **AI-agent-with-work-context setup**.

## 3. Breaking through the "OneNote wall"

*Didn't Microsoft OneNote already offer sufficient structure?* It was, after all, **perfectly fine for my personal use**: I casually accumulated agenda items for recurring meetings, I leaned on the structured actions I kept in those notebook section pages — but my **own thinking was the sole engine** behind that note-taking's payoff.

For an AI agent, that OneNote is a closed silo. Its proprietary format formed a "OneNote wall" that my agent couldn't independently browse, parse, or navigate. My drive for reusable AI context required a **liberation of knowledge into the "Markdown era".**

[Markdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markdown) isn't just an aesthetic preference — it guarantees that your professional history, as **universal, plain-text files**, is easily navigable by your agent like a file system. No binary, proprietary format, just raw text with conventions. *[LaTeX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LaTeX) for beginners, quoi*.

- **The OneNote era (rigid/siloed):** proprietary binary formats, closed ecosystems, high friction for agent navigation.
- **The Markdown era (transparent/universal):** universal plain-text files, high transparency, and a dynamic graph architecture in which agents can read and query raw text directly.

## 4. "Working memory" versus "long-term memory" architectures

*"Great,"* you'll say, *"I'll just convert my entire work context into that machine-readable form. We dump the whole Markdown archive on that AI agent and let it chew through it."* Ouch — there goes the reasoning quality of your AI deployment. That's called **context bloat**, and that pitfall undermines your AI returns.

The key to quality AI reasoning is a **layered memory structure**:
- **Long-term memory** — the full collection of your raw notes and their rewrites, on disk. As in the earlier [Hello World — Tinkering in Public](/posts/tinkering-in-public) post, [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md) is an ideal tool for this.
- **Working memory** — a task file as a deliberately assembled, limited context slice — specific sources and links — scoped to one active task.

And where there's memory, there are **nodes and connections: a note [graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graph_(discrete_mathematics))**. As a human you tap into the power of Obsidian (where this note graph is called a **[Vault](https://help.obsidian.md/Getting+started/Create+a+vault)**) to navigate that note graph via the Obsidian UI; your AI agent taps into it via the Obsidian Command-Line Interface ([CLI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command-line_interface)) and possibly the Model Context Protocol ([MCP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_Context_Protocol)).

This **use of the note graph** naturally isn't limited to *read access*. Your task agent answers questions and distills insight and output that **flows back** into your note graph, so that **your long-term memory grows in value with every agent deployment**.

*So what is that working memory / task file?* It consists of the following parts:
- focusing on specific notes — e.g. one project, or just a handful of consideration notes (**session scoping**);
- targeted links and backlinks between notes in your vault (**sources**);
- tight instructions on success criteria and boundaries (**goal/scope**); and
- note file properties like type, status, date for filtering (**metadata**).

## 5. Security has to be verifiable

*Doesn't using Claude like this throw your entire Vault **up for grabs**?* No — not with the right settings.

I personally chose the **Claude for Work Team Premium plan**. My notes involve company-related information after all, which implies information-security standards ([CIA triad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_security#Key_principles), [ISO 27001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO/IEC_27001), [GDPR Art. 28](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-28-gdpr/)/[32](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-32-gdpr/)).

What's notable about the Claude Team plan, summed up in three points:
- **Contractual and legal basis** — the plan provides Anthropic Commercial Terms including a Data Processing Addendum (DPA) compliant with GDPR Art. 28, including Standard Contractual Clauses (SCC) for data transfers outside the EEA.[^1]
- **Model training and data use** — my Claude sessions are, by default, never used to train AI models.[^2][^3] This is contractually fixed in the Commercial Terms and requires no opt-out or manual setting.
- **[GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/) compliance** — Art. 28 (data processing agreement) is covered via the Commercial Terms, Art. 32 (technical & organizational measures) via the DPA and SOC 2/ISO 27001 certifications, and Art. 33/34 (breach notification) in case of incidents.

The Claude Team plan further provides the following **security measures**:
- *[SOC 2 Type II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_and_Organization_Controls)*: independent audit of security controls (availability, confidentiality, integrity). Report available under NDA.
- *[ISO 27001:2022](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001)*: international certification for information security management. Fully certified since 2025.
- *[ISO/IEC 42001:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html)*: AI-specific governance standard; demonstrates responsible management of AI systems.
- *[CSA STAR Level 2](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/star)*: Cloud Security Alliance certification for cloud providers.
- *[NIST 800-171](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/171/r3/final)*: attestation for protecting controlled unclassified information.
- *Annual penetration tests*: external security tests; reports available via the Trust Center under NDA.
- *Encryption in transit*: TLS for all data transfers.
- *Encryption at rest*: AES-256 for stored data.
- *Central admin*: user management and billing from a single dashboard.

A detailed comparison of the four plan tiers across 21 security and compliance criteria is in the [appendix](#appendix--detailed-security-comparison) at the end of this post.

**AI agent deployment scoping** — these measures lead me to handle the following information types as follows:
- *internal documents without personal data* (architecture documents, technical specs): fine to process.
- *productivity tasks* (summarizing, translating, drafting): fine to process.
- *personal data, employee data*: don't process — requires Zero Data Retention, which only Claude Enterprise provides.
- *strictly confidential board documents*: same — don't process.

## 6. Guard as code — why "be careful" is not a safety net

The nice thing about deploying AI is that there's only a **short distance between "lesson indicated" and "lesson learned".**

For instance, I fairly quickly switched from editing my Obsidian vault through the local file system to managing it via the Obsidian CLI. However, Claude didn't immediately realize that `obsidian delete --help` doesn't produce help instructions but instead immediately deletes a note. Even though Claude played it safe, this still amounted to an incident.

<ZoomableImage src="/images/posts/beyond-the-chatbot/guardrail-in-tekst-vs-code-slide.png" alt="Comparison of prompt-based guardrails, which operate on trust and fail silently, versus code-based guardrails that intercept and block commands before execution" />

Safety requires deterministic, technical enforcement. I realize that through **"guard as code"**:
- **Enforced read-only access:** technical hooks and scripts that block write actions to source data, regardless of what the agent's prompt says.
- **The human gatekeeper:** the agent prepares the draft or the commit, only I actually execute it.

A safety net that exists only in text ("be careful") is no safety net. A safety net that blocks the action itself, is.

## 7. Conclusion: the human spark

In software engineering we know that technology changes, improves, and gets replaced. The move from OneNote to Markdown is, in that sense, just a small technical step. The old habit of **disciplined note-taking** was the real leap. And your AI agent can of course add excellent value on top of that discipline.

So, above all, question your own habits. *How do you structure your thoughts and your work context?* *Is the gap between human intent and machine execution, for you too, still to be bridged by **structuring and digitizing** your own work context?*
